# Target Audience Personas — Irreverent Marketing

---

## Persona 01 — The Chrome Purist

**Name:** Marcus, 38
**Location:** Phoenix, AZ / Las Vegas, NV (desert-adjacent)
**Income:** $75–120k
**Vehicle:** Restored 1968 Chevrolet Camaro + daily driver

### Who He Is
Marcus has spent 6 years restoring his Camaro. He knows the difference between a compound and a polish. He's watched 400 hours of detailing YouTube. He doesn't buy cheap. He buys **right**.

### What He Wants
- Products that match the obsession he already has
- A brand that speaks his language without talking down
- Proof that his car can look the way it does in his head

### What Moves Him Creatively
- Extreme close-ups of chrome surfaces
- Before/after reveals (especially if the "before" is dramatic)
- Cinematic treatment — he wants his car culture elevated, not mocked
- Retro aesthetics that honor the golden era of American cars

### Platform Behavior
- Instagram: saves product posts, follows detailing accounts
- YouTube: long-form tutorial consumer
- Facebook: in car restoration groups

### Buying Trigger
Sees a product shot so good it makes his own car feel inadequate. Buys to close that gap.

## Persona 02 — The Aesthetic Buyer

**Name:** Jordan, 29
**Location:** Los Angeles, CA / Austin, TX
**Income:** $55–85k
**Vehicle:** 2019 Mustang GT or equivalent "statement" car

### Who She Is
Jordan bought a car that turns heads and she knows it. She's not deep into detailing technique but she cares deeply about how her car looks in photos. She's style-first, function-second.

### What She Wants
- A product that *looks* as good as it works
- Content she'd want to repost
- To feel like she's in on something cool, not being sold to

### What Moves Her Creatively
- High-fashion editorial framing applied to car culture
- Desert / landscape photography aesthetics
- Strong brand identity that feels like a lifestyle, not a product
- 9:16 video she can share to Stories

### Platform Behavior
- Instagram: heavy Reels consumer, saves aesthetic posts
- TikTok: discovery platform, shares to Instagram
- Pinterest: visual research for car/lifestyle content

### Buying Trigger
The creative makes her car feel like a character, not a vehicle. She wants that story.

---

## Persona 03 — The Retrofuturist

**Name:** Daniel, 44
**Location:** Scottsdale, AZ / Nashville, TN
**Income:** $120k+
**Vehicle:** Newer luxury or sports car + interest in classic restoration

### Who He Is
Daniel loves the *idea* of the past filtered through the lens of now. He watches old car shows, buys modern reissues of vintage goods, and appreciates brands that understand design history. He has disposable income and taste.

### What He Wants
- Brand credibility and design intelligence
- Products that feel worthy of the machines he loves
- Copy that treats him as culturally literate

### What Moves Him Creatively
- Retrofuturist visual language (exactly what this campaign delivers)
- Long-form storytelling — he'll read the caption
- Photography that looks like it cost real money
- References that reward knowledge (Jet Age, NASA, concept cars)

### Platform Behavior
- Instagram: curated, quality-over-quantity consumer
- Facebook: less active but convert-ready
- Email: opens brand emails if voice is right

### Buying Trigger
The brand feels like it understands what a car *means*, not just what it does.

---

## Psychographic Thread

All three personas share a core belief:
> **A car is not transportation. It is identity made physical.**

Marketing that understands this earns immediate trust. Marketing that misses it gets scrolled past.
