## ICP 1 — Marcus (Primary · 35–55)

**Age:** 35–55  |  **HHI:** $80K–$160K  |  **Family:** Yes — his dad had a heart attack at 58

Marcus works in sales, trades, or management. His doctor recently said his cholesterol was "borderline" or blood pressure was 128/82. He's been trying things — fish oil, a multivitamin, AG1 for two weeks before canceling. He's not afraid of $42 — he's afraid of wasting $42 on something that tastes like grass and disappears into his cabinet. He doesn't say "I want to optimize my health." He says "I want to not end up like my dad."

**How to win him:** Name the organ he's worried about in the first 3 seconds. Give him a real ingredient and what it does (CoQ10 = mitochondrial fuel for the heart). Show someone who looks like him having a genuine taste reaction. Make $42 feel obvious.

**Creative instinct:** Specificity over aspiration. "Heart, liver, and kidneys" hits differently than "overall wellness."

## ICP 2 — Gym Guy (Secondary · 25–40)

**Age:** 25–40  |  **Entry point:** Already buying Atlas Creatine or Planta from Ambrosia

Already in the ecosystem. Buys on brand trust, not organ education. Adds Nektar as a stack extension — not a replacement. Walter P.'s review is the entire ad for this person: *"Mixed my Atlas creatine with it and it still tastes great. You definitely get your bang for your buck."*

**How to win him:** Short. Trust transfer. Show the Ambrosia stack. Let existing brand loyalty do the work — no need to educate on organs.

**Creative instinct:** The cross-sell is the creative. One post, one product shot, one review quote.

## ICP 3 — Health-Conscious Woman 🟢 Green Field (Underserved · 30–50)
