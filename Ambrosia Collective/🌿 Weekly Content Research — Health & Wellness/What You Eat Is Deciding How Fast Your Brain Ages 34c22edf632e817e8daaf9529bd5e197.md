**Setup (3–10 sec):** We've been treating mental health almost entirely as a chemistry and psychology problem. But the research on food and the brain is rewriting that entire story — and most clinicians are behind the evidence by about a decade.

**Point 1 (10–20 sec):** Ultra-processed foods — the ones with ingredient lists longer than a sentence — drive chronic neuroinflammation. And chronic neuroinflammation is now one of the leading suspected mechanisms behind both treatment-resistant depression and early-onset cognitive decline. You are not just what you eat. **Point 2 (20–30 sec):** BDNF — brain-derived neurotrophic factor — is the compound your brain uses to grow new neurons, repair damaged ones, and maintain the connections between them. Most people eat almost none of these on a regular basis.

**Point 3 (30–45 sec):** A Mediterranean-style diet — emphasizing fish, vegetables, legumes, nuts, and olive oil — has been associated with a 35% lower risk of cognitive decline over 10 years. That is a larger effect size than most Alzheimer's drugs currently in clinical trials. The fork is the most underrated medical intervention in psychiatric and neurological medicine.

**Bridge (45–55 sec):** This week: two servings of fatty fish. A daily handful of blueberries or mixed dark berries. Swap whatever refined oil you're cooking with for extra-virgin olive oil. Those three changes cover more BDNF-stimulating ground than most supplement stacks on the market.
