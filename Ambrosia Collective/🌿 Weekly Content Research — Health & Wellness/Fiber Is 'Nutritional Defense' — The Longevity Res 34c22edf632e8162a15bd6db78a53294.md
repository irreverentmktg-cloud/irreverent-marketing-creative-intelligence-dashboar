## 🎬 Video Script

**Hook (0–3 sec):** Longevity researchers have stopped calling fiber 'roughage.' They're calling it 'nutritional defense' now — and once you understand why, you'll never skip it again.

**Setup (3–10 sec):** Most people think of fiber as something that helps you go to the bathroom. That framing is about 30 years out of date. Fiber is a systems-level health intervention.

**Point 1 (10–20 sec):** When you eat fiber, gut bacteria ferment it into short-chain fatty acids — butyrate, propionate, and acetate. These compounds don't just stay in your gut. They travel into your bloodstream, regulate immune cell behavior, modulate systemic inflammation, and directly influence how your cells respond to insulin. Fiber is a microbiome-to-body signaling mechanism. Why it matters: chronic low-grade inflammation is the common pathway behind every major age-related disease.

**Point 2 (20–30 sec):** Every major long-term cohort study — from the Nurses' Health Study to the European EPIC trial — finds that high dietary fiber intake is independently associated with lower all-cause mortality. Not just lower colon cancer risk. Lower all-cause mortality. The people who eat the most fiber live longer than the people who eat the least, across every population studied.

**Point 3 (30–45 sec):** Most fiber benefits come from variety, not just quantity. Insoluble fiber feeds one set of bacteria. Soluble fiber — from oats, legumes, apples — feeds another. Fermentable fiber — from garlic, onions, Jerusalem artichokes — feeds the bacteria that produce the most butyrate. Rotating your fiber sources is one of the simplest ways to increase microbiome diversity.

**Bridge (45–55 sec):** This week: add one new fiber source you don't currently eat regularly. Lentils if you don't eat beans. Oats if you don't eat them. Cooked-and-cooled potato if you want resistant starch. One new source, every week, for a month.

**CTA (55–60 sec):** Save this. Follow for longevity science that goes deeper than the headlines.
