## 🎬 Video Script

**Hook (0–3 sec):** Two cups of beans a day — that's it. And your gut bacteria will thank you for the rest of your life.

**Setup (3–10 sec):** Most people eat for taste. But your gut microbiome eats for survival — and beans are exactly what it's starving for.

**Point 1 (10–20 sec):** Beans are loaded with prebiotic fiber — the kind your gut bacteria ferment into short-chain fatty acids. These are anti-inflammatory compounds that protect your gut lining, reduce chronic bloating over time, and lower your risk of colon cancer. Why it matters: gut lining integrity is the difference between nutrients absorbing properly and low-grade inflammation leaking into your bloodstream daily.

**Point 2 (20–30 sec):** Beans also contain tryptophan — an amino acid your body uses to manufacture serotonin. About 90% of your serotonin is produced in your gut, not your brain. More beans means more building blocks for the chemicals that regulate your mood, sleep, and stress response.

**Point 3 (30–45 sec):** One cup of cooked black beans has 15 grams of fiber — more than half the daily recommended intake most Americans never hit. The key: start with half a cup of rinsed canned beans. Give your microbiome two weeks to adapt before you judge the results. The initial gas is your gut bacteria adjusting — not a sign it's not working.

**Bridge (45–55 sec):** Tonight: rinse half a cup of canned beans and add them to whatever you're already eating — a salad, a grain bowl, scrambled eggs, even soup. Just start somewhere small.
