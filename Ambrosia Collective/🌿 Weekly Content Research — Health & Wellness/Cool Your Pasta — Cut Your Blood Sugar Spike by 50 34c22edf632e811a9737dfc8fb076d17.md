## 🎬 Video Script

**Hook (0–3 sec):** Cold pasta has fewer usable carbs than hot pasta. This isn't a trick — it's food chemistry, and it's been replicated in peer-reviewed research.

**Setup (3–10 sec):** If you're trying to manage blood sugar, the cooking method matters less than what you do after you cook. The timing changes everything.

**Point 1 (10–20 sec):** When you cool cooked starches — pasta, rice, potatoes — the starch molecules reorganize into a structure called resistant starch. Your digestive enzymes can't break it down the same way they break down hot starch, so the blood sugar spike you'd normally get is dramatically smaller. Why it matters: repeated blood sugar spikes are the mechanism behind insulin resistance, energy crashes, and accelerated biological aging.

**Point 2 (20–30 sec):** One study found that cooled and reheated pasta caused 50% less of a blood sugar rise compared to freshly cooked pasta. That's not a marginal difference — that's clinically relevant for anyone dealing with insulin resistance, pre-diabetes, or even just afternoon energy crashes.

**Point 3 (30–45 sec):** Here's the bonus: resistant starch also acts as prebiotic fiber for your gut bacteria. So cook-cool-reheat doesn't just improve your glycemic response — it simultaneously increases microbiome diversity. One habit, two mechanisms.

**Bridge (45–55 sec):** The practical version: cook a big batch of rice, pasta, or potatoes tonight. Refrigerate overnight. Reheat tomorrow. You get the same volume of carbs with a fraction of the blood sugar impact — zero extra cost, zero extra time.

**CTA (55–60 sec):** Save this — it's one of the most under-discussed nutrition hacks that has real research behind it. Follow for more.
