## ICP Avatars

### ICP 1 — “Derek” (Primary · The Experienced Lifter · 27–40)

Trains 4–5x/week. Protein, pre-workout, possibly a generic monohydrate. Reads Reddit fitness, follows Marc Lobliner / Mike Rashid, shops Tiger Fitness. He's taken creatine before — probably generic. He's open to upgrading but skeptical that any creatine is meaningfully better than the $15 bag.

**Why he's skeptical:** He's been burned on supplement marketing before. “More ingredients” doesn't convince him. Mechanism does.

**How to win him:** Give him the mechanism. Pürest Creatine® = ATP, explosive. Acetic acid = glycogen, endurance. Two real systems. Price-match to BPN proves you’re not charging a premium for marketing. The BJJ Round 4 testimonial is the most differentiated social proof in the dataset — use it.

**Creative instinct:** Specificity. “Up 15 lbs on my bench in 3 weeks” > “crush your goals.”

---

### ICP 2 — “Tyler” (Secondary · The Endurance Athlete · 25–42)

Runs, cycles, CrossFit, BJJ, OCR. Currently takes electrolytes, beta-alanine, maybe beetroot. Has written off creatine as “a bodybuilder thing.” Never considered it applied to him.

**Why he ignores creatine:** His mental model of creatine is ATP — explosive, short-duration power. He cares about glycogen (he just doesn’t call it that). He cares about mile 8, round 4, the last interval.

**How to win him:** Category reframe first. “Creatine was built for lifters. Atlas was built for everyone who goes longer.” The acetic acid story is the unlock. Sport-specific creator — BJJ, CrossFit, cyclist — not a bodybuilder.

**Creative instinct:** Address the belief before the product. Once you change his category model, the product sells itself.

---

### ICP 3 — “Ashley” (Underserved 🟢 Green Field · The Creatine-Curious Woman · 24–38)

Completely unaddressed in current Atlas creative. Zero female UGC has ever existed for this product. Has seen creatine content on TikTok. Follows “girls who lift.” Knows she probably should take creatine. Is blocked by three myths no brand has debunked for her in paid media: hair loss, bloating, bulking.

**Why she hasn't bought:** The internet has told her creatine is a male supplement that might cause hair loss and will definitely bloat her. Her skepticism is rational, given the information available to her.

**How to win her:** Female UGC creator (lifting 2–3 years, not a physique competitor). Lead with myth debunk — hair loss study is 2009, rugby players, DHT not actual hair loss. Bloat is from cheap creatine with fillers. Close on six-week outcome.

**Creative instinct:** Unsell the myths before you sell the product. Creative that opens with “I was scared of creatine” converts this buyer; creative that opens with “unleash your potential” bounces.

**Priority:** This is the highest-opportunity untapped segment in the pipeline. First mover window is open now.

Lead with ingredient integrity: "We don't cut corners on what goes in the tub." These buyers still want the best — the window is open.
3. **Ashley is the single biggest untapped segment in Ambrosia's entire portfolio.** Zero Atlas creative speaks to her. Zero competitor creative speaks to her. TikTok women's creatine content is the fastest-growing creatine content segment in 2025. First-mover advantage is real, finite, and closing.
4. **Tyler's category doesn't know creatine is for them.** Every single Atlas creative opportunity with Tyler starts with reframing the category — not selling the product. Once he believes acetic acid = glycogen = endurance fuel, the product is obvious.
5. **Gummy lab failure is the most public-domain steal angle in the category.** Data is public. Failures are documented. Atlas's Creapure® certification is a direct credibility counter. Act before gummy brands pivot messaging.
6. **Container fill perception is 100% of Atlas's 1–3★ reviews.** The product works. The packaging perception blocks repeat purchase. One pre-emption creative removes this permanently.
